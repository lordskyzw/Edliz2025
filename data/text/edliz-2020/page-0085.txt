Patients with severe or moderate acute malnutrition AND complications
should be admitted for inpatient care. Complications should be managed
according to national protocols for different age groups.
Provide routine medicines in both Phases | and II per national protocol
as appropriate for age and complications of patient.
Hypoglycaemia and hypothermia usually occur together and are signs
of infection. Check for hypoglycaemia whenever hypothermia
(axillary<35.0°C; rectal<35.5°C) is found. Frequent feeding is important
in preventing both conditions.

The glucose or sucrose solution (1 rounded teaspoon of sugar in 3.5
tablespoons water), can be given orally or by nasogastric (NG) tube. If
the child is unconscious, lethargic or convulsing give IV sterile 10%
glucose (5mi/kg), followed by 50m! of 10% glucose or sucrose by Ng
tube. Then give starter F-75.
Treatment of Infection in Severely malnourished
children
The usual signs of infection, such as fever are often absent in severe
malnutrition. Therefore, presume infection in severe malnutrition and give
routinely broad-spectrum antibiotic(s) AND measles vaccine if child is >
6months and not immunized (delay if the child is in shock).
