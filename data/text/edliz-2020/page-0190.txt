Contraindications for PrEP
e HIV positive status
° Unknown HIV status
° Recent exposure (in the past 72 hours)
e Evidence or suspicion of HIV primary infection (characterised by a
flu-like illness)
e Allergy to any of the PrEP medicines
° Unwilling/unable to adhere to PrEP
° Known renal impairment
° Estimated creatinine clearance of <60 cc/min
Follow up and Monitoring of PrEP clients
e After initiating PrEP, the client should be reviewed after one month
to monitor adherence and side effects as well as resupply of
medicines, subsequent clinic visits should be every three months
. Perform an HIV antibody test every three months and document
negative HIV status
° For women, perform a pregnancy test based on clinical history.
Pregnancy is not a contraindication for PrEP use
e Review the client's understanding of PrEP, any barriers to
adherence, tolerance to the medication as well as any side effects
° Review the client's risk exposure profile and perform risk reduction

e Evaluate and support PrEP adherence at each clinic visit

Discontinuing PrEP
PrEP can be discontinued in the following circumstances:
e On seroconversion (HIV positive)
e Intolerable toxicities and side effects associated with PrEP use
e Changed life situations resulting in lowered risk of HIV acquisition
e Social adverse events associated with PrEP
e Poor adherence despite efforts to improve daily pill taking
e When a personal choice is made to stop PrEP. Such individuals
should be linked to other preferred HIV prevention options
