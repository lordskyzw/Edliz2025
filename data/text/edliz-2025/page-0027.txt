Medicines in the elderly
This chapter has more recommendations covering the management of osteoporosis,
frailty and dementia. The cautions to be observed when prescribing for the older
patient have been enhanced.
Rheumatological and Joint conditions
The preferred inflammatory marker is CRP as opposed to the ESR.
Hydroxychloroquine (has less toxicity) has replaced chloroquine for the
management of SLE. Hydroxychloroquine is given as 400mg daily, unlike
chloroquine which was used as 200mg daily. Whichever of these antimalarials is
being used, give it Monday to Friday and omit at the weekend; this reduces the
risk of the patient getting retinal toxicity.
Mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) (S medicine) is more effective than azathioprine as
a steroid sparing immunosuppressant and has less adverse events.
Gout- when starting allopurinol in treatment naive patients, begin with a low dose
of 50mg daily and increase every 2 weeks. See further recommendations in this
chapter.
If using biologics e.g. Rituximab (S medicine), do a CXR and blood tests to
exclude TB, HIV, Hepatitis and C. Patients receiving rituximab must be pre-tested
for B-lymphocyte adequacy and for herpes zoster immunity.
The recommended adult dose of methotrexate is 15-25mg once weekly, not the
lower levels that we have mentioned in the past version. All these patients must
also receive oral folate Smg twice or thrice weekly, but the folate should not be
taken on the same day as the methotrexate.
