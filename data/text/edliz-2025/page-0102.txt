General Note:
Medicines should be avoided, if possible, throughout pregnancy, and especially
during the first trimester. However, medicines may be required for a number of
conditions commonly encountered during pregnancy; medicines which are
appropriate and safe are covered in the sections that follow. At the end of the
chapter is a list of those medicines which should be avoided or used with
caution during pregnancy or lactation. The Family Planning Guidelines of
Zimbabwe provide comprehensive guidance. Refer to them wherever possible.
Instructions for use, contraindications etc, are also found in the manufacturers'
package inserts.

Hormonal Contraception

Important: Ensure a free and informed choice by providing counselling on the

advantages and disadvantages of contraceptive methods. Oral, injectable and

implants do not protect against HIV. For added protection there is need to use a

barrier contraceptive such as a male or female condom.

Checklist for those not trained in family planning:

all these questions are ‘no’, the woman may be given any oral contraceptives. If

any of the answers are ‘yes’, a doctor must see her first.
