Medicines Interactions with Oral Contraceptives
Medicines reducing the effect of oral contraceptives
Caution is needed when prescribing any of the following medicines to any woman
taking oral contraceptives; they reduce the effectiveness of the oral contraceptive
and pregnancy is more likely:
e Anti-convulsant: carbamazepine, ethosuximide, phenobarbitone, phenytoin,
primidone.
e Antibacterials: rifampicin, rifabutin, rifapentine
e Anti-retroviral: efavirenz
If the medicine is only going to be used for a short time the woman should be
advised to take extra contraceptive precautions (for example, condoms) for the
duration of the therapy, and seven days after treatment. If the medicine is to be
used on a long-term basis the woman should be advised to use another suitable
method of contraception.
Medicines which are made less effective by oral contraceptives
Doses of medicines may need to be increased, with careful monitoring:
e Anticoagulants
e Anti-convulsant (phenytoin)
e Antidepressants (imipramine)
e Anti-hypertensive agents (methyldopa)
Corticosteroids
e Hypnotics, sedatives or other CNS depressants (diazepam, phenothiazines)
e Anti-asthmatic agents
