Antenatal Management
The woman should be managed and reviewed more frequently by both a specialist
obstetrician and physician. In the antenatal period avoid fluid overload, anaemia
and infection. Any infection should be treated aggressively with the appropriate
antibiotics.
Treatment:
See treatment of heart failure under cardiovascular disease chapter.
Anticoagulants: long term anticoagulation (e.g. for valve replacement) — using
warfarin should be avoided in the first trimester. Alternatively, use heparin or
enoxaparin for the first 12 weeks, and change back to warfarin between weeks 13
— 37. After 37 weeks change back to heparin until after delivery. Warfarin can be
commenced 24hrs after delivery.
Labour in cardiac patients (Refer to the Cardiovascular chapter)
Cardiac disease patients should not be induced. They usually have easy vaginal
deliveries, which can be assisted by forceps delivery or vacuum extraction to avoid
stress.

Contraception:
At 6 weeks after delivery, use the progesterone only oral contraceptive or
injectable medroxyprogesterone acetate or progesterone implants.
