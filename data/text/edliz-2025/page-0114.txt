Hypertension in Pregnancy
Women who develop hypertension during pregnancy (after 20 weeks) have
pregnancy-induced hypertension (PIH), which is a potentially serious condition
possibly requiring early or urgent delivery (see below).
Pregnant women who have essential hypertension may also develop superimposed
PIH and merit the same treatment. Methyldopa is the recommended anti-
hypertensive throughout pregnancy.
Prevention of hypertensive disorders through screening for and use of calcium
carbonate daily until delivery is recommended.
CAUTION: Avoid diuretic medicines during pregnancy.
Calcium supplementation
1.5g daily from 20 weeks of gestation
Low dose Aspirin
150mg Aspirin taken once daily from 12 weeks up to 36 weeks in high-risk women
with at least one high risk factor or at least two moderate risk factors
