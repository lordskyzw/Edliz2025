Herpes Simplex Virus
In immunosuppressed persons, primary and recurrent herpes simplex lesions are
usually more severe, more persistent, more symptomatic and more resistant to
treatment. The early vesicular lesions (blisters) may be transient or never seen. In
immunosuppressed patients, any erosive mucocutaneous lesion should be regarded
as herpes simplex until proven otherwise.
Patients need to be counselled about the following:
e The recurrent nature of the lesions, as well as their likely persistence,
e The infectiousness of mucocutaneous or genital herpes.
e Care of local lesions: keep them clean with frequent (2 to 3 times a day) washing
with water and keeping them as dry as possible.
e In very severe cases or immunocompromised patients, acyclovir should be given.
e Bacterial superinfection may complicate lesions and will require antibiotics
e Suppressive therapy may be required for frequent (more than 4 to 6 episodes per
year) recurrent HSV infections. After 1 year, the situation may be reviewed to
either continue or change to episodic treatment or discontinue and assess the
clinical situation.
