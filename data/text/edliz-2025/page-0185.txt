Psychosocial criteria for initiating ART
Consider the following psychosocial criteria when initiating ART:
e Has the recipient of care been adequately counselled and informed about ART?
e Is a treatment partner available and/or has disclosure been made to that treatment
partner? (strongly encouraged)
e Is there an easy method of following up on the recipient of care?
e Is the recipient of care ready to take medication indefinitely?
Situations where it may be necessary to defer ART initiation
A recipient of care may be deferred (delayed) from starting therapy if he or she:
e has cryptococcal meningitis,
e needs further psychosocial counselling (e.g., for substance & or alcohol abuse
problems),
e has TB (defer starting ART for at least 2 weeks),
e needs further information on HIV and AIDS,
e Is very ill and unable to swallow oral medication (palliative care is then offered
to such a patient).
*SUCH PATIENTS SHOULD BE OFFERED CONTINUED MONITORING AND
CLOSE FOLLOW-UP AS WELL AS COUNSELLING SO THAT ART CAN BE
COMMENCED AT AN APPROPRIATE TIME*
ADHERENCE TO ART
Efforts to support adherence should start before ART initiation and should include
basic information about HIV, the ARV medicines, expected adverse events,
preparations for long-term ART. Effective adherence support interventions include
client-centred behavioural counselling and support, support from peer educators
trained as “expert clients,” or community adolescent treatment supporters (CATS)
and mobile text messaging. Other interventions involve encouraging people to
disclose their HIV status and providing them with adherence tools such as pill
boxes, diaries, and patient reminder aids. During follow-up, recipients of care
should be assessed for adherence to whatever treatment plan has been agreed upon.
Age-appropriate disclosure beginning in childhood fosters adherence to treatment
in adolescents.
