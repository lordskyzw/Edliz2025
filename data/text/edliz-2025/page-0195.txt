CARE OF AN HIV-EXPOSED INFANT

Initial care

Care for HIV-exposed infants should include the following:

e Make sure HIV-exposed infants are entered into the “HIV exposed follow-up
register”.

e All HIV-exposed infants should have a Nucleic Acid Test performed from birth
or at the earliest possible time thereafter if birth-testing is missed.

e Cotrimoxazole prophylaxis should be given from six weeks of age until the HIV
status of the infant is known. If HIV infection is confirmed, continue
cotrimoxazole and commence on ART.

e Monthly follow-up visits are recommended, but more frequent visits may be
needed if problems are detected.

During these visits the following services should be provided:

e Growth monitoring and promotion

e Developmental assessment

e Age appropriate vaccinations

Counselling on infant and young child feeding:

e Counselling and support for the HIV ipositive mother to adhere to ART is
crucial.

e Exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months of life, introducing appropriate
complementary foods at 6 months and continuing breastfeeding to 24 months
and beyond.

e Weaning should not be abrupt, but rather should be gradual over a one-month
period.

e Breastfeeding mothers should immediately seek treatment for mastitis, cracked
nipples, infant mouth lesions and thrush to reduce the risk of MTCT.

e Immunisations should be given according to the national guidelines. The BCG
vaccination should still be given at birth, but BCG should not be given to
children with symptomatic HIV infection.

e Always look for and treat opportunistic infections.

MANAGEMENT OF AN HIV-POSITIVE CHILD USING ARVS

Infants and young children have an exceptionally high risk of poor outcomes from

HIV infection. The goal of ART for children is to increase survival and decrease

HIV-related morbidity and mortality.
