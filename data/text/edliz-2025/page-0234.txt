While patients may have documented risk factors that predispose them to DR-TB,
patients may also present with a primary episode of DR-TB with no apparent risk
factors. Risk factors for developing DR-TB (or worsening the pattern or resistance)
include (but are not limited to) the following:
e Patients who remain (or again become smear positive) during or after
completing a TB treatment regimen.
e Improper treatment of TB/DR-TB which includes treatment interruptions,
incorrect doses for the patient’s weight
e Contact with a DRTB patient
e HIV infection
e Residence in high burden DRTB regions
The management of DR-TB or XDR-TB cases is rapidly changing with emerging
evidence on more patient friendly and effective regimens. The drugs and drug
grouping used in the treatment of DR-TB is shown in table 12.7. For the latest
guidance and practice recommendations on the management of DR-TB, please
refer to the Clinical Management of Drug-Resistant Tuberculosis Guidelines.
