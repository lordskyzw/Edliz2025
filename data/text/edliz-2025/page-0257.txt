Malaria prophylaxis for:

Intermittent Preventive Treatment in Pregnancy (IPTp)

@ Pregnant women in regions of moderate to high transmission.

Chemoprophylaxis in this group in based on an assumption that every pregnant

woman in a malaria-endemic area is infected with malaria and has malaria

parasites in the blood or in the placenta. The medication is given at treatment

doses and at prescribed intervals.

e Three tablets of SP (each SP tablet contains Sulphadoxine 500 mg and
Pyrimethamine 25 mg) are given from 13 weeks (after quickening).

e Give SP to all pregnant women at each scheduled ANC visit up to time of
delivery.

e The doses should be given at least 4 weeks apart

e SP should be given as directly observed therapy (DOTS) of three tablets

e SP can be given on either an empty stomach or with food

e SP should NOT be administered to women receiving Co-trimoxazole
prophylaxis due to an increased risk of adverse effects

e It is recommended that weekly folic acid also be given to pregnant woman
taking IPTp. (This is done in conjunction with the Reproductive Health
Department).

TREATMENT OF MALARIA

All antimalarial medicines should be administered only to confirmed cases

(Confirmation is done by RDT or microscopy). However, in children less than five

years treatment may be initiated whilst awaiting blood results provided other

causes of fever have been clinically excluded.

Malaria blood slides MUST be taken in the following cases:

e All admitted malaria patients.

e Patients with severe/ complicated malaria,

e Patients with suspected treatment failure,

e All referrals

e Suspected mixed infections e.g. those patients with a recent travel history to
countries where other malaria species are reported.

e All cases where Co-artemether has been used in the preceding 2 weeks

e All RDT confirmed cases in malaria elimination areas
