ACUTE RESPIRATORY INFECTIONS IN ADULTS

Outpatient management

For acute respiratory infections in children see the paediatrics chapter.

Key Points:

These are usually viral infections, so antibiotics are not required.

Treatment is symptomatic (e.g., fever reducers, hydration, rest).

Other respiratory infections (Including pneumonia and other severe lower

respiratory infections)

e The approach to management may be influenced by the patient’s HIV status.
Always exclude TB and PCP. Loss of weight, productive cough for > 3 weeks,
night sweats and a fever require TB screening i.e. sputum tests and/or CXR.
Take a history of the duration of symptoms, sputum production (colour,
haemoptysis and volume), constitutional symptoms of anorexia, weight loss,
night sweats, and pyrexia. Ask about pleuritic chest pains.

e If tuberculosis is unlikely and the patient’s condition does not warrant admission,
treat the infection with:
