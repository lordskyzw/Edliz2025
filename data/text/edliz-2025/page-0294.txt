Principles of pharmacological management of hypertension
Monotherapy is usually inadequate therapy for most people with hypertension.
Initial therapy with a combination of two drugs (at low to medium range doses)
should now be considered usual care for hypertension. Use of fixed dose
combinations is encouraged whenever possible/available to improve compliance.
Exceptions: those patients with mild (Grade I) hypertension and no additional
cardiovascular risk factors/comorbidities, or in some frailer old or very old patients,
in whom more gentle reduction of BP may be desirable.
Note: Methyldopa and propranolol are no longer recommended for the treatment
of hypertension except in special circumstances.
A simplified drug treatment algorithm: A combination of a thiazide/thiazide-
like diuretic and calcium channel blocker is the preferred initial therapy for most
patients. Other combinations: Calcium Channel Blocker (CCB) and ACEi/ARB or
thiazide/thiazide-like diuretic and ACEi/ARB are alternative first line
combinations.
