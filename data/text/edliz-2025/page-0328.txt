BLEEDING OESOPHAGEAL VARICES

Commence treatment immediately, before confirmation of diagnosis by

endoscopy/barium meal. Resuscitate completely, and only refer when patient is

stable:

e Insert large IV cannula to transfuse and to replenish blood volume. Avoid saline
unless no alternative.

e Correct raised INR/PT with fresh frozen plasma and vitamin K

© Routinely give 3 generation cephalosporin (ceftriaxone) during acute bleeding.

e Sedate [avoid opiates]:

diazepam iv C V_ 5-15mg as necessary
ceftriaxone iv Cc Vi ig twice daily 5 days

e Treat concurrent encephalopathy as above.

e Aspirate nasogastric tube hourly

e Refer for early endoscopy.

e If bleeding persists, refer, urgent referral for endoscopy

e Refer to specialist.

@ Give Propranolol prophylactically indefinitely:

ASCITES OF CHRONIC LIVER FAILURE

Perform diagnostic paracentesis if possible. Check for white cell count and total

proteins.

e Restrict salt intake and fluid intake to 1 litre/day. Give potassium supplements if
hypokalaemic. This regimen plus bed rest is enough to induce a diuresis in some
patients.

e Aim for weight loss of 0.5 kg per day. Any more could lead to hypovolaemia
and precipitate liver failure.

e Polymorph cell count in ascitic tap >250 cells/uL defines spontaneous
bacterial peritonitis (SPP) irrespective of other results of microscopy and
culture. IV ceftriaxone is indicated. Alternatively, oral ciprofloxacin may be
given in absence of vomiting, shock and prior exposure to quinolones.
