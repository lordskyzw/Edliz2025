URINARY TRACT INFECTIONS (UTI) IN ADULTS
Cystitis
Usually presents with dysuria, urinary urgency and frequency, and suprapubic pain,
but note that in men dysuria more commonly indicates a sexually transmitted
infection (STI). Always exclude an STI. With UTI, urine is often cloudy and
smelly. Where possible diagnosis should be made with leucocyte dipstick,
microscopy or culture.
e Treat with:

e Ifstill symptomatic after 3 days, refer.
Urinary tract infections in children (including acute pyelonephritis)
Recommend urgent urine microscopy and culture, and the immediate
commencement of antibiotics if:
1.Age <3 months
2.Specific urinary symptoms in children of 3 months to 2 years of age
3.Non-specific symptoms in children of 3 months to 2 years of age at high risk
of serious illness.
In children 3 years of age and older, a urine dipstick should be used to initially
diagnose UTI.
Management

e Intravenous (IV) antibiotic for at least 3 days before switching to oral antibiotic
