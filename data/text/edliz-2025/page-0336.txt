ACUTE NEPHRITIC SYNDROME
Rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis (RPGN) is a renal emergency!!! It is a
disease of the kidney characterized clinically by a rapid decrease in the glomerular
filtration rate (GFR) of at least 50% over a short period, from a few days to 3
months.
Presence of features of glomerulonephritis (i.e. facial or generalised oedema,
haematuria plus oliguria and hypertension) and rapid deterioration of kidney
function should be managed as RPGN with immediate initiation of intravenous
steroids and urgent referral to a tertiary hospital.

POST INFECTIOUS NEPHRITIS -
Usually presents with facial or generalised oedema plus oliguria and hypertension.
Check urine microscopy for active sediment and do U & Es daily. There may be a
recent history of tonsillitis, arthralgia, skin rashes/ infection.
e Promote diuresis with small doses of oral furosemide (40-80mg once daily). If

response obtained put on a regular dose.
e If post-streptococcal infection is suspected give:

Medicine Codes Adult dose Frequency Duration

e Treat hypertension conventionally. Children need early intervention for elevated
