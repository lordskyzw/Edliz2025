NEPHROTIC SYNDROME IN ADULTS

Diagnosed where there is generalised oedema, hypoalbuminaemia and proteinuria

(>3gm/day). Dipstick should show at least protein ++. To quantify the proteinuria,

you can request a urine albumin: creatinine ratio. Check urine microscopy and

U&Es. Estimate the GFR (creatinine clearance). Weigh patient at each review.

Exclude SLE, HIV and Hepatitis B or C or diabetes. Renal biopsy should be

considered where available Consult a nephrologist. The nephrotic syndrome may

respond to an ACEI and may be steroid sensitive.

e Promote diuresis using:

Caution: Excessive use of frusemide may precipitate renal failure and large

doses of frusemide may cause hearing loss. Therefore, check U&Es regularly.

Measure urea and electrolytes. Restrict fluid to 1 litre per day until diuresis

occurs. If oedema is gross and no response, consider adding prednisolone

particularly if the urine sediment is benign (i.e. no red cells or casts).
