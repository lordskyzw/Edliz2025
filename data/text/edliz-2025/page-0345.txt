Medicines and Dialysis
Dialysis may remove significant quantities of some medicines e.g. penicillins,
aminoglycosides, cephalosporins, chloramphenicol, metronidazole, methyldopa,
anti-TB therapy, quinine. Therefore, give supplementary doses following a
haemodialysis session. The dialysis team will advise on supplementary doses.
END STAGE RENAL DISEASE/CHRONIC DIALYSIS
End Stage Renal Disease: Stage 5, GFR <15ml/min: This is said to have occurred
if a patient needs dialysis for at least 3 months and thus the need for chronic
dialysis may be more likely.
Chronic dialysis clients have to be selected carefully as renal replacement therapy
is expensive and not easily accessible. Clinical and psychosocial selection criteria
for the chronic dialysis programme are applied and each individual patient should
be discussed with the dialysis team. Some psychosocial issues may exclude the
patient from the chronic dialysis programme. The goals of providing this therapy
should be addressed adequately with patient and relatives. Current dialysis does
entail some out-of-pocket expenses for the patient and family. Thus, ensure there
has been adequate pre-counselling prior to starting patient on the chronic dialysis
program.
Chronic peritoneal dialysis and haemodialysis may be available in your city or
town but discuss with the specialist before transferring patient to the dialysis unit.
Renal transplantation
There is currently no renal transplantation in Zimbabwe, but some patients have
had live donor related renal transplantation outside this country at their own
expense or that of their medical aid society.
