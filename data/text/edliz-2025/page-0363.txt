Oral Care:

e@ Good oral hygiene should always be encouraged, and patients should have
annual dental check-ups with a dentist or dental therapists.

Gastrointestinal upset:

e e.g. vomiting, diarrhoea or constipation must be checked often as they are an
indicator of complications. Metformin can cause GIT upset and can be replaced.

Diabetic Clinics

e Are useful to focus care even at District Hospital level. Six monthly reviews
should include eye checks, checking for peripheral neuropathy, checking for
foot problems, oral care, sexual dysfunction and BP.

SPECIAL PROBLEMS IN DIABETICS

Pregnancy

e Oral anti-diabetic agents can be used (with Specialist guidance) and very strict
glycaemic control is necessary. See the chapter on Obstetric and Gynaecological
conditions.

Infections and Other Major Illnesses

e Both types of diabetics (Type 1 and Type 2) may need to be given an increased
dose of infusion or Basal Bolus Insulin (see insulin therapy in adults). In Type 1
diabetes NEVER stop insulin, even if the patient is unable to eat. After a
hypoglycaemic event correct with 20mls of 50% dextrose and restart the insulin
at the appropriate dose. Do not stop insulin in a type 1 diabetic.

e Insulin as a BASAL BOLUS REGIMEN is the anti-hyperglycaemic treatment
of choice in patients admitted to Hospital. There is no role for subcutaneous
sliding scale in inpatient diabetes management. For critical patients in HDU and
ICU a variable rate intravenous insulin infusion (VRIII) can be used,

Hypoglycaemia and Hypoglycaemic Coma

e Always educate patients and document in the notes about hypoglycaemic
symptoms (hunger, sweating, irritability, etc)..
