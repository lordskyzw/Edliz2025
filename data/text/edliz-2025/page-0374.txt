INFECTIONS OF THE NERVOUS SYSTEM

The usual presentation of meningoencephalitis is with:

e headache and fever

e and/or altered level of consciousness

e and/or neck stiffness

e and/or focal neurological signs

e and/or seizures.

In infant’s irritability, lethargy and failure to suck are important signs. Neck

stiffness is an unreliable sign under one year of age, but the detection of a bulging

fontanelle supports the diagnosis.

Differential diagnosis

Headache and fever only: look for cause of fever in other systems (for example,

chest, respiratory tract; urinary tract, etc.). Always exclude malaria

MENINGITIS

Management of suspected meningitis (fever + headache + altered alertness) at

District level (or higher):

e Urgent lumbar puncture.

e Blood slide for malaria parasites.

e If diagnosis is in doubt DO NOT perform a lumbar puncture. Refer to a
higher level.

e Contraindications to lumbar puncture: deeply unconscious + focal signs; one
pupil large and unresponsive; papilledema (if fundoscopy available); rapidly
falling level of consciousness. These are indications for referral to a tertiary care
centre.

e Lumbar puncture should be considered mandatory, and, preferably, when the
condition is first suspected since Cryptococcal meningitis must always be
excluded.
