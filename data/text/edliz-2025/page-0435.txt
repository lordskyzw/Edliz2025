PREVENTION OF BLINDNESS

80% of blindness can be prevented by:

proper diet (Vitamin A and proteins)

personal and environmental hygiene

e measles immunisation

e early treatment of eye diseases by qualified health personnel including cataract
surgery and early diagnosis and treatment of glaucoma

© appropriate management of STIs in pregnant mothers and their sexual partners

e carly referral of serious eye diseases and injuries

e tetracycline eye ointment in the new-born child’s eyes (Crede Prophylaxis)

e early detection and appropriate management of diabetes — stringent control of
blood sugar. All diabetic patients should have screening of eye complications by
an ophthalmologist regularly starting from diagnosis of the disease.

early detection and appropriate management of hypertension

e creation of community awareness on the dangers of using herbal medicines in
the eye

© persistent conjunctival growths should be referred to an ophthalmologist early,
in case it is due to Ocular Surface Squamous Neoplasia

e HIV positive patients must be screened for eye complications by an ophthalmic
nurse or ophthalmologist if symptomatic

patients with allergic conjunctivitis must be discouraged from rubbing eyes and
appropriate treatment should be used e.g. antihistamines. Avoid use of topical
steroids since they can cause glaucoma in steroid responders and early cataract
formation.

“Healthy bodies, healthy eyes!”

Organic headaches such as migraine and cluster headaches do NOT occur

because of eyestrain. See the chapter on Nervous System Conditions for

management of these conditions.

Excessive use of eyes does NOT harm them, and “bad eyes” do NOT result from

overuse.

MANAGEMENT OF EYE CONDITIONS

Diagnose and refer urgently most of the following conditions:

e unexplained vision loss; or visual disturbance

e glaucoma: high pressure in the eye
