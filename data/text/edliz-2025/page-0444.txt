GLAUCOMA (CHRONIC OPEN ANGLE)
Glaucoma is defined as a progressive optic neuropathy characterised by retinal
ganglion cell loss of the retinal fibre layer resulting in characteristic changes in the
optic nerve head. Features include loss of visual field, cupping of optic disc and
plus or minus elevation of intraocular pressure. Types include Primary open angle
glaucoma (POAG) which is usually the commonest where aqueous drainage is
obstructed despite open angles, its asymptomatic and has a familial and racial
tendency, ocular hypertension and low-tension glaucoma. Diagnosis is based on:
Elevated intraocular pressure (IOP), Cupping of Optic Disc, Visual Field loss and
Gonioscopy. Risk factors include race / ethnicity, increasing age, positive family
history, thin central cornea etc.
Medical treatment comprises the following groups of drugs:
e Prostaglandin Analogues,
e Adrenergic Drugs,
e Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors,
e Parasympathetic Agents,
e Rho Kinase Inhibitors,
e Combined Medications and
e Hyperosmolar Agents.
Mechanisms of action differ.
A specialist must first confirm the diagnosis of glaucoma. The treatment can then
be repeated according to the specialist’s prescription, provided the patient is
referred to the specialist every 3 months for review. The specialists should tailor
therapy for POAG to the individual needs of the patient including the target IOP,
medicine effectiveness, cost and likelihood of compliance. Starting with a single
medicine first is always recommended unless IOP is extremely elevated in which
case two or more complimenting medicines can be used. First line therapy may
include: Prostaglandin Analogue, Beta Blocker, Alpha Adrenergic Agonist and
Topical Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitor.
