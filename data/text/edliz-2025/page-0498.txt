MANAGEMENT OF PAIN IN CHILDREN

See also the section in the Cancer module.

e Pain in children needs careful and regular assessment as children may not
complain of pain. Babies also experience pain and may require analgesics.
Parents are good judges of their child’s pain.

e@ When available/ necessary suppositories should be used.

elf pain is intractable refer for specialist management e.g. regional block or
wound infiltration or palliative sedation

e Infants < 6 months of age require lower initial opioid dosing, approximately 25-
50% of the opioid doses provided.

e Utilize developmentally appropriate symptom assessment tools to determine
presence and severity of pain.

e Current WHO analgesic ladder for children has 2 steps (mild and severe). Mild
opioids (codeine and tramadol) containing medicines are not encouraged in
chronic pain control in children <12 years due to hepatic insufficiency.

e In reference to codeine:

oln the foetus, CYP2D6 activity is absent or less than 1% of adult values. It
increases after birth, but it is estimated to be no higher than 25% of the
adult values in children below five years. Therefore, the analgesic effect
is (very) low or absent in neonates and young children.

o Percentage of poor metabolizers can vary in ethnic groups from 1% to 30%,
resulting in ineffectiveness in large numbers of patients, including
children.

e Conversely, individuals who metabolize codeine quickly and extensively are at
tisk of severe opioid toxicity, given the high and uncontrolled conversion of
codeine into morphine.

e In reference to tramadol:

ethere is currently no available evidence for its comparative effectiveness and

safety in children.
