GENERAL NOTES

Due to physiological changes and altered pharmacodynamic response of target

organs the elderly are more susceptible to adverse medicine reactions.

Most patients over 70 years old have an average of 5 medical complaints and

polypharmacy is common. Therefore, medication should be reviewed frequently

(every 3 months).

Principles of management in elderly

e Provide simple, once or twice daily regimens wherever possible.

e Give clear instructions on how medicines are to be taken.

e Where possible ask relatives to supervise medicine administration.

e Suppositories or liquid formulations should be prescribed where swallowing is
difficult.

e Use reduced doses

e Avoid nephrotoxic medicines

Principles of prescribing for older people

The care of older people is complex for various reasons:

e Physiological complexity secondary to frailty, multiple long-term conditions,
and polypharmacy

e Diagnostic uncertainty secondary to non-specific presentations (see geriatric
‘giants’) and atypical signs/symptoms

e Higher prevalence of co-existing psychological, cognitive, functional, and social
issues

e Complex ethical considerations, particularly with regards to autonomy and
cognitive capacity

e With advancing age and chronic conditions comes limited prognosis, requiring
individualised ‘Advance Care Planning’ which considers each patient’s priorities
and preferences to guide their care and treatment.

Such complexity requires multi-domain assessment and management planning —

this is often termed ‘Comprehensive Geriatric Assessment? (CGA), when

performed in full by a multidisciplinary team.
