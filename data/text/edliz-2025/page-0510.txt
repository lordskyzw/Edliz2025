Anti-coagulation for atrial fibrillation in older people

Atrial fibrillation (AF) confers risk of clot formation in the atria, which can travel

to the cerebral circulation causing embolic strokes. To minimise this risk, anti-

coagulation is often recommended, generally with a direct oral anti-coagulant

(DOAC, e.g. Apixaban, Edoxaban, Rivaroxaban), and where these are not

available, with warfarin. However, anti-coagulation creates bleeding risk.

Therefore, the stroke-risk (quantified using the CHA2DS2-VASc score) is

balanced against the bleeding risk. Falls risk is rarely a reason not to anti-coagulate

as the substantial benefits largely outweigh the small risks.

CHA2DS82-VASc score for AF stroke risk:

e Age: <65 (0 points)/65-74 (1 point)/ >75 (2 points)

e Sex: Female (1 point)/ Male (0 points)

e History of heart failure: No (0 points)/ Yes (1 point)

e History of hypertension: No (0 points)/ Yes (1 point)

e History of stroke/TIA/VTE: No (0 points)/ Yes (2 points)

e History of vascular disease (IHD, PVD): No (0 points)/ Yes (1 point)

e History of diabetes: No (0 points)/ Yes (1 point)

CHA2DS2-VASc score 0=low risk, no need for anti-coagulation

CHA2DS2-VASc score 1=moderate risk, consider anti-coagulation

CHA2DS2-VASc score >2=high risk, anti-coagulation recommended

Analgesia

Older people can be prescribed analgesia according to the WHO analgesic ladder,

with a few key considerations:

e Reduce the dose of paracetamol from maximum 1000mg QDS to 500mg QDS if
weight <S0kg

e Non-steroidal anti-inflammatories (NSAIDS, e.g. naproxen, ibuprofen,
diclofenac, indomethacin) are best avoided, and if used should be used with
extreme caution, due to substantially increased risk of GI complications and
nephrotoxicity. Where absolutely necessary, consider co-prescription of a
gastro-protective PPI (e.g. omeprazole, lansoprazole), and monitor renal
function.
