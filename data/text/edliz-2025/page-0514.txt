EDLIZ 2025

Lifestyle advice:

1.Recommend a healthy, balanced diet (including protein), minimize alcohol and
avoid smoking.

2.Ensure sufficient dietary calcium (e.g. kapenta fish, dairy, green cabbage, green
beans).

3.Ensure skin is regularly exposed to the sun (for vitamin D).

4. Encourage a combination of regular weight-bearing and muscle strengthening
exercise.

Anti-resorptive treatment:

Any postmenopausal woman, or a man age > 50 years who:

1. Has had a fragility fracture (especially a vertebral, humerus or hip fracture); start
anti-resorptive treatment promptly (because re-fracture risk is highest
immediately after a fracture).

2. Is starting >5 mg/day Prednisolone or equivalent over the next 3 months (i.e.
2450mg in total over a 3-month period), start concurrent anti-resorptive
treatment.

3.Offer antiresorptive therapy with oral Alendronate or intravenous
Zoledronate as first-line treatments to reduce fragility fracture risk.

4.Consider Hormone replacement therapy (HRT) for the treatment of
postmenopausal fragility fracture risk in younger post-menopausal women (aged
< 60 years) who have low risk for adverse malignant and thromboembolic
events.

5. Regularly review patients' tolerance of, and adherence to, oral drug treatments.

6.Long-term treatment is often required, because osteoporosis is a long-term
condition for which there is currently no cure. Plan to prescribe oral
alendronate for at least 5 years, or intravenous zoledronate for at least 3
doses and then re-assess fracture risk.

Longer durations of treatment will be needed in those who are older (age >70

years), have had a hip or vertebral fracture, are on high-dose oral glucocorticoids

[27.5 mg/day of prednisolone or equivalent over 3 months], or have a further

fragility fracture during treatment.
