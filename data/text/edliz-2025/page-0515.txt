Delirium

Delirium is a syndrome, not a disease. It is defined in the Diagnostic and Statistical

Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM IV) as having four key features:

1. Disturbance of consciousness, characterised by inattention.

2. Change in cognition or perception not accounted for a pre-existing or evolving
dementia.

3. Development acutely over a short period (usually hours to days), and fluctuating
over the course of each day.

4.A history supportive of an organic underlying cause, such as a medical illness,
infection, metabolic derangement, medication initiation or withdrawal, pain, or
constipation. Environmental factors can also precipitate a delirium, especially
when the individual is already at risk (e.g. they have dementia, frailty, and/or
sensory impairment).

With delirium, patients can be hypo or hyperactive, and this can fluctuate.

Disorientation, reduced concentration, dysphasia, dysarthria, visual hallucinations

are all possible features. There are multiple simple tools to assess for delirium,

including the Confusion Assessment Method (CAM), and the 4 As Test (4AT).
