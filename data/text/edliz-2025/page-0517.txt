Incontinence
There are four types of urinary incontinence: stress incontinence (due to weakness
of structures around the urinary outlet, often following childbirth), urge
incontinence (due to high bladder pressure, often caused by overactive bladder
syndrome), overflow incontinence (due to bladder outlet obstruction, which causes
an overfull bladder to overflow in an uncontrolled manner), and mixed
incontinence (mixed pathology — usually a combination of stress and urge
incontinence).
Non-pharmaceutical management should include:
> Consideration of hyperglycemia and hypercalcemia as causes of polyuria,
> Medication review, especially of diuretic use,
» Provision of advice on fluid intake with avoidance of salty, caffeinated, and
fizzy drinks,
>In both men and women, pelvic floor retraining exercises, bladder training,
double voiding, and approaches to reduce sleep disturbance,
> In men, techniques to empty the urethral bulb to limit terminal dribbling.
If available through private prescription, mirabegron is the medication with the
lowest anti-cholinergic burden — trial for 2-3 months and stop if improvement of
symptoms. Avoid oxybutynin and other similar anti-muscarinics (listed in FRIDs
table above).
