Oral hypoglycaemics (see Diabetes section in Metabolic and Endocrine
Conditions Chapter)
The elderly are at an increased risk of hypoglycaemia with Glibenclamide and
should be prescribed Gliclazide.
Hypnotic / Sedatives
Benzodiazepines (e.g. Diazepam) significantly impair cognitive function and
should not be used. Sedation should be achieved with:

lorazepam po B_ E_ 0.5-lmg at night intermittently
Major tranquillisers
It is essential to define and remove the underlying cause of agitation e.g. infection
or hypoxia. Once this is done and if tranquillisation is still considered necessary,
the options are:

Medicine Codes Adult dose Frequency Duration
haloperidol po B V_ 0.5-2mg twice daily review

or haloperidol im B V_1-5mg twice daily review
Always start with the lower dose if possible. Usually haloperidol 0.5 mg twice
daily which is often enough. Avoid chlorpromazine and fluphenazine decanoate
where possible as major irreversible side effects may occur.
Anti-diarrhoeals
The elderly are prone to spurious, or overflow diarrhoea from chronic faecal
impaction. History of ‘warning’ symptoms or drug side effects is important. No
diarrhoea in the elderly should be treated with anti-diarrhoeal medicines before an
adequate physical examination i.e. per rectal examination, has excluded impaction.
In such cases a high fibre diet, regular enemas and a stimulant such as senna or
bisacody] will relieve the problem.
