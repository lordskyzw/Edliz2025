CORROSIVE SUBSTANCES

e.g. battery acid, drain cleaners, oven cleaners, laundry powders, strong

hypochlorite or ammonia solutions, carbolic acid and phenols, pool acids, dish

washing detergent.

e Immediately dilute by the administration of fluid. Water or milk (for acids) may
be used. Avoid excessive oral fluid to prevent vomiting.

e Do not induce vomiting since the corrosive agent will cause further damage.

e Note: with hydrofluoric acid (HF):

(a)Systemic poisoning may cause circulatory failure associated with
hypocalcaemia, hypomagnesemia and/or hyperkalaemia (may require rigorous
supportive and symptomatic therapy; monitor CVS especially the heart)

(b)For topical exposure (note that systemic poisoning can occur after topical
exposure to HF), after flushing skin with water, put calcium gluconate gel on
affected area and massage it continuously until pain goes (about 15 minutes). If
calcium gluconate is not available, soak affected area in solution of magnesium
sulphate (Epsom salts)

e [Immediate use of salts MAY PREVENT deep burns, but once the acid has gone
below the skin they are less effective].

INHALED POISONS

e.g. liquid polishes, chloramine (produced by mixing hypochlorite and ammonia),

chemical gases, chemical sprays

e Remove patient from further exposure by carrying to fresh air immediately.

e If breathing is impaired give artificial respiration.

e Follow first aid measures listed above.
