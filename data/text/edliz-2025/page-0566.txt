SNAKE BITE

First Aid for Snake Bite

e Calm and reassure the patient. Get them to lie down.

e If venom has been spat in the eye, wash liberally with water for at least 15
minutes.

e Apply a pressure bandage (not a tourniquet) firmly around the limb, starting
from the bite site and moving upwards. This allows blood flow to the limb but
prevents lymph return and absorption of poison.

e Splint the limb to prevent movement that would increase absorption of poison.

e Get the patient to a hospital with facilities to give antivenom. Reassure them on
the way and be prepared to give artificial respiration if required.

e Do NOT:

cut the wound

use a tourniquet

give electric shock to the site

rub or massage the wound site.

In hospital

e Remove the pressure bandage

e Give analgesia and:

Medicine Codes Adult dose Frequency Duration
tetanus toxoid C_ V-_ see chapter on immunisation

e If no signs of envenomation, observe for 24 hours (5 days if boomslang bite)
then discharge.

e Only if signs of envenomation (bleeding, signs of neurotoxicity, severe swelling
of bitten limb) give antivenom:

Medicine Codes Adult dose Frequency Duration

*snake B_ E 40m! [all ages]. Repeat as required. (serious bites from
antivenom, neurotoxic snakes may require up to 100ml)
polyvalent iv

*Caution: Antivenom use may lead to serious anaphylaxis (if anaphylaxis occurs,

manage as started in the relevant section on anaphylaxis). Please also note that the

administration of a test dose is no longer recommended.
