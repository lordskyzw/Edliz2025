Indications

e Induction and maintenance of anaesthesia;

e Subanaesthetic dosage may be used to provide analgesia for painful procedures,
e.g. dressing of burns.

e Induction agent of choice in shocked patients

Contraindications

e Hypertension,

e Raised intracranial pressure,

e Psychiatric disorders.

Cautions

e Hallucinations may complicate recovery, particularly when ketamine is given
for maintenance; this problem can be reduced by use of diazepam or midazolam.

e There may be excessive salivation, so use of atropine should be considered.

e Respiratory obstruction and depression may occur, though less commonly than
with other anaesthetics

ANAESTHESIA GASES AND INHALATIONAL ANAESTHETICS

Nitrous Oxide

This anaesthetic gas reduces the requirement for more potent anaesthetics and is

also analgesic, given in a concentration of 50-70% in oxygen.
Medicine Codes Dose
nitrous oxide B V Titrate to effect for analgesia, induction or

maintenance of anaesthesia

Contraindications

e Patient with an air-containing closed space, (e.g. pneumothorax, middle ear
abnormalities, bowel obstruction) since nitrous oxide will expand such space
with deleterious effect.

Cautions

e The main danger in the use of nitrous oxide is hypoxia; at least 30% oxygen
must be used.
