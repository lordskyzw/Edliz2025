Local Anaesthesia & Conduction Anaesthesia
This includes local infiltration, peripheral nerve block, spinal and epidural
anaesthesia and analgesia.
e Equipment for resuscitation should be readily available, and intravenous access
must be established before administering a local anaesthetic.
e Factors influencing the safe dosage of local anaesthetics include patient’s age,
weight, physical status, vascularity of the area to which the medicine is applied.
e Adrenaline should not be added to local anaesthetic applied to body parts with
end arteries eg toes, fingers and penis

e Spinal and epidural blocks should only be attempted by persons trained in these
techniques.

e Spinal and epidural blocks should only be conducted where a vasopressor (e.g.
ephedrine, phenylepherine) is available to treat possible hypotension.

e Preservative-containing solutions should not be used for spinal anaesthesia.
