BREAST ECZEMA
This is a common condition which is usually confused with cracking nipples in
breastfeeding mothers. However, exclusion of breast cancer is important as in
Paget’s disease. Breast eczema is most likely to be bilateral unlike Paget’s disease
which is usually unilateral. Where appropriately diagnosed, breast eczema can
then be managed as per guidelines in the skin conditions chapter. Where there is
doubt of diagnosis a biopsy of the affected skin is important.
THYROID CONDITIONS
Patients who present with a goitre need to be assessed properly. The assessment
should focus on assessing their thyroid state (thyrotoxicosis, euthyroid or
hypothyroid) and exclude complications which might be linked to malignant
change e.g. upper air ways compression, hoarse voice in recurrent nerve invasion,
retrosternal extension or superior vena cava compression, rapid growth, fixation,
enlarged lymph nodes.
Treatment for thyrotoxicosis is laid out in the endocrine conditions chapter.
Surgical intervention is indicated in thyrotoxicosis if
e Not responding to medical therapy.
e Thyrotoxicosis not responding to radioiodine.
e Thyrotoxic patients where antithyroid drugs and radioiodine are contraindicated.
e Confirmed malignancy.
e Suspected malignancy where confirmation is not possible before surgery e.g. in

follicular carcinoma.
e Goitre causing compressive complications on the airway, major vessels and

nerves.
