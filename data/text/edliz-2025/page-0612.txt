General Notes
Since the thalidomide disaster, voluntary reporting of adverse drug reactions (ADRs)
has become important in monitoring the safety of medicines. ADR reporting can also
help to identify irrational prescribing, bad batches of a medicine and problems specific
to particular patient groups.
For the purpose of reporting an adverse drug reaction (ADR), a medicine is defined as:
Any substance administered to man for the prophylaxis, diagnosis or therapy of disease,
or the modification of physiological function.
An adverse reaction to a medicine is defined as:
Any response to a medicine which is noxious and unintended, and which occurs at
doses normally used in humans for the prophylaxis, diagnosis, or therapy of disease, or
for the modification of physiological function.
This includes herbal and traditional medicines.
ADRs to be reported to the Medicines Control Authority of Zimbabwe (MCAZ)
All ADRs to marketed medicines or medicines added to the Essential Medicines List,
even when all the facts are not available or there is uncertainty that the medicine caused
the reaction should be reported. These include:
e Allergic reactions
Side effects
e Interactions
¢ Medicine abuse/ misuse/ non-approved uses
e Effects of withdrawal
e Effects in an infant of a nursing mother
e Effects possibly as a result of exposure of the mother or the foetus during pregnancy
Examples include anaphylaxis to penicillin, Steven- Johnson Syndrome (SJS) in
patients given cotrimoxazole, itching due to chloroquine, ete.
Issues with medical products quality should also be reported to the MCAZ. Healthcare
practitioners and consumers are encouraged to report any product defects to MCAZ
using the report on medicinal product defect or problem form attached as Annex (x)
which is available on the MCAZ website or on request via email to mcaz@mcaz.co.zw .
These include:
e Suspected contamination
© Questionable stability
e Defective components
e Poor packaging or labeling
e Therapeutic failures
